# Historical grounding

Treat the image style and the historical claims as separate decisions. Rounded drawings can depict either a researched event or an invented scene; neither appearance nor a social-media style tutorial proves historical accuracy.

For a factual brief, identify the period, region and claim being illustrated. Consult appropriate primary material, museum collections or scholarly references when details matter. Link the sources used and record which details they support. Do not invent a quotation, date or statistic merely to make the opening stronger.

For a fictional brief, label the story as fiction and state the broad period inspiration. Keep uncertain clothing and architecture as interpretations. Avoid calling a reconstruction an exact portrait. An image prompt can still be useful when no historical source has been supplied, provided it does not make unsupported precision claims.

Resolve these visual decisions before the shots:

- Garment layers, silhouette, fastening, headwear and footwear for each role.
- The face color, hair and personal features that must remain identifiable.
- Tools, furniture, transport and building materials visible in the action.
- Color roles and lighting appropriate to the chosen setting.
- Which details are sourced, inferred or deliberately invented.

Do not use a universal costume for every region or century. Preserve an explicitly requested outfit while pointing out a material mismatch if the user also requires factual accuracy. A style conversion should not silently change the subject's skin tone, identity, social role or the story's meaning.

A historical performance or sound cue also needs care: a modern score may be an editorial choice, but it should not be described as an authentic period recording. A fictional action needs no invented historical attribution.
